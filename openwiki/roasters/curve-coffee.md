# Curve Coffee Roasters

## Overview

Curve began in 2016 when Tereza and Jon left London for the Margate coast with
a second-hand roaster, and is now a fifteen-person team roasting for Kent and
beyond — "Delicious coffee. Positive impact." is the site's one-line mission.
The company name comes from roasting itself: the temperature-vs-time curve
plotted during every roast. The Squarespace storefront sells rotating
"Feature Filter" and espresso coffees with harvest-season sourcing
(e.g. ACC Pink Bourbon from Cauca, Colombia and Lalesa Black Honey from
Ethiopia); subscriptions are handled through Subbly. They also run a café in
Margate, having started out roasting from the back of the Cliffs community café.

## Sustainability

- **1% for People & Planet**: a minimum of 1% of *all* revenue is donated to
  community-centred projects, split between coffee-producing communities and
  the Margate/Kent community; donations are itemised in a published
  Impact Report 2024/25.
- Producer projects funded: **The Stumping Project** in Ethiopia (tree
  rejuvenation with TechnoServe and import partner Falcon Coffees) and the
  **Drying Beds Project** in San Lorenzo, Colombia (twelve drying beds built
  with export partner Siruma over the past two years).
- UK-side: supports **Dane Valley Woods** (a volunteer-run community woodland
  on a former landfill site) and **Rise Up. Clean Up.** (Margate beach cleans).
- 250g and 1kg bags are 80% post-consumer recycled LDPE (recyclable with soft
  plastics); a paper-based 250g bag is being explored — per their FAQ.
- Long-standing member of the World Coffee Research check-off programme:
  5p donated per 1kg of green coffee bought through participating importers.

## Sourcing & Transparency

- Buys in line with harvest seasons; the rotating range changes as fresh-crop
  coffees are released, and sold-out coffees are replaced with the closest
  alternative rather than stocked year-round.
- Buys its year-round House Espresso from the San Lorenzo community, Colombia
  continuously since 2019 — their largest annual green purchase. Key export
  partner is Siruma in Colombia; newer partners include Finca Churupampa and
  the La Coipa village group in Peru (since 2024). Most coffees come from
  smallholder farms of 0.5–15 ha (average 1–3 ha).
- Deliberately skips organic certification: per their site, they prioritise
  partners with regenerative practices and technical support programmes over
  certification labels.

## Roasting & Equipment

- **Loring Falcon S15** closed-system roaster — chosen for precise, immediate
  heat control and reduced emissions.
- Every batch is logged, colour-read and tasted, with roast-curve data linked
  to cupping results.
- Filter roasts sit light-to-medium (brightness and fruit), espresso roasts
  medium (body and sweetness); recommended rest of 7+ days after roast.

## Philosophy & Quirks

- The roaster's name *is* the method: "What the hell is a roasting curve?"
  opens their roasting page — the obsession with plotting and shaping roast
  curves is the brand's origin story.
- "Feature Filter" is their rotating single-origin filter line; the range is
  intentionally seasonal and ever-changing.
- FAQ tone is candid — including a frank explanation of why they don't chase
  organic certification.
